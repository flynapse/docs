## Scales

- **Severity** is the reviewer's own number, moved out of the column they called "Tier" (FO-R2, FO-R3 and FO-R4
  used 0 = a content leak / 1 = a defect / 2 = a coverage gap, sometimes as "1-class"; FO-R0 and FO-R1 used P1/P2
  labels only). `*` means the assembler derived it:
  - `0*` a content or personal-data leak that ships;
  - `1*` a correctness or availability defect, or a gate, lock or guard-integrity defect;
  - `2*` a coverage gap (a blind guard or an unguarded property, with no live instance);
  - `3*` docs, prose or process.

  `—` means the claim holds and there is no defect. Where a thread spans rounds, each round's value is named.
- **Tier** is §2.3a's scale, assigned by the assembler:
  - **0** — a verifiable property that an **independent reviewer** saw a named guard fail on under a named
    mutation or plant, with nothing later refuting it. A reviewer re-running its own plant after the fix counts.
    It never goes to Fable.
  - **1** — consequential but reversible.
  - **2** — irreversible or estate-shaping: what an exported span or log record CARRIES (the withholding rules are a
    wire contract every repo's dashboards and reviewers read), the declared leak set, the boot refusal, publish and
    deploy order, an owner ruling.

  A tier-2 decision stays tier 2 even when its code is settled: the guard proves the implementation, not the
  judgment. **An implementer's own mutation proof never buys tier 0.**
- **Chunk.** Tier 2 goes to F1. Tier 0/1 rows whose subject is what leaves the process (exception text, URLs,
  secrets, PII) go to F1. Guard mechanics, the ordering latch, test infrastructure, cost, hygiene and the range's
  own commits go to F2. Deferred, owner-owed, accepted debt, prose, process and other-repo pointers go to F3.
- **Claim state (at review)**, as that reviewer found it: SETTLED, ASSERTED, OPEN, REFUTED or PARTIAL.

## Findings, ranked

The assembler's ranking of what an auditor needs, not a new review.

### 1. Two tier-0 leaks were found in this repo's OWN withholding code, and both are reviewer-proved closed

- **FO2-01.** The G.112 body rule turned the boundary into an arbitrary-file read: `frames_only` re-read
  `linecache` for every `File "<path>", line N` header it found in FREE TEXT, so a logged subprocess stderr naming
  `secret.env` exported that file's first line. Closed at `1cdf318` (headers only, both paths, no `linecache`
  import), and FO-R3 re-attacked it with 13 traceback shapes on all four log routes and a planted `secret.env`:
  nothing came out. M-FAILURE-HOME later moved the renderers into `failure.py`, where r5 and r6 proved the same
  property again (R5-19, R6-18).
- **FO3-01.** `uvicorn.access` logs `path?query` at INFO and sits in utils' intercepted loggers, so core's anonymous
  `GET …/invitations/preview?token=<tenant>.<secret>` was exported verbatim through the real route; G.116 only
  recognised `scheme://` tokens. Closed at `e8d1df1` (per-token recognition), and FO-R4 re-drove it through real
  uvicorn h11, httptools and websockets. The owner's answer to the part no seat can cover (ALB/CloudFront access
  logs) is M-INVITE-FRAGMENT: the token leaves the URL altogether.

### 2. The withholding rules are the estate's wire contract, and four of them are still moving

change everywhere. Four decisions in this file are tier 2 with settled code:

  filters on `url.path` dead (FO2-06, since fixed).
- Exception text withheld from every exported log record, stacktrace reduced to frame headers (G.112, FO2-03).
- URL secrets in log TEXT judged by parameter NAME (G.116, FO2-07) — the one rule in this package that reads text,
  and the one that every later round kept re-shaping (FO3-02…08, FO4-02…08, then r5 R5-14…17 and r6 R6-14…16,
  and `4b48507` now awaiting r7).
- The declared leak set (FO3-11): what the rule cannot see, written down in the plan and re-derived by each round.
